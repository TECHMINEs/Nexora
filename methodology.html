Optional reference

How the model works

Nexora uses two illustrative factories to show how risk, loss, and action decisions connect. The calculation engines use the supplied sample records and explicit assumptions. This page explains their meaning and limits in one place.

Risk score

The model combines hazard intensity, exposed assets, vulnerability, and operational importance. Verified controls reduce the result. The score helps compare the illustrative hazards; it is not a probability or a forecast.

Likely financial loss

Each scenario estimates physical damage, lost stock, business interruption, extra expenses, recovery, and supply disruption. Gross loss has no control benefit; residual loss reflects verified controls; the planned case assumes committed actions are verified.

Actions and evidence

Actions are ranked using impact, likelihood, urgency, and feasibility. A proposed or purchased action does not automatically reduce modelled loss. Its benefit appears only when its state and evidence meet the model rules.

Insurance gap

The model compares an exposure estimate with policy limits and terms. This is a discussion aid, not a coverage determination. A licensed adviser should review exclusions, deductibles, and policy wording.

What the demo cannot tell you

  • The factories, scenario values, telemetry, and replay outcomes are illustrative, not live operational records.
  • Hazard scores are comparative planning scores, not event likelihoods or return periods.
  • Loss values depend on assumed damage, downtime, inventory, and policy terms; they are not valuations or claims estimates.
  • Official emergency warnings and instructions always take precedence over this prototype.
  • Some sample source records have limited validation or uncertain reuse rights. Unlicensed inputs are gated out of outputs.
Technical calculation notes

Risk: hazard × exposure × vulnerability × criticality × (1 − verified mitigation), normalised to a score out of 100.

Business interruption: downtime × daily contribution, with separate extra expense and supply-chain terms.

Action priority: impact × likelihood × urgency × feasibility, scaled to 0–100.

Confidence: model metadata tracks source quality and verification method. Small samples and self-declared values lower confidence; they do not become precise because the UI displays a number.

The complete assumption and source records remain in the repository data files for audit and development.
